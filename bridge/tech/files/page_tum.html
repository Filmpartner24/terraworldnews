                  First-year welcome at the open-air event “Welcome@TUM”

                  Record-breaking start: More students and more international than ever

                  For the winter semester 2026/27, TUM expects record student numbers. It is projected to have a total of around 53,800 students, including 16,750 newly enrolled students. At the open-air event “Welcome@TUM” on the Garching campus, the university welcomed its new students. The event was supported by Freunde der TUM e.V. (TUM Association of Alumni and Friends). According to preliminary figures, 48.8 percent of them are international.

                  Connecting sensors at traffic lights and in cars

                  Using AI to respond more quickly to traffic hazards

                  Researchers at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) and the BMW Group have combined sensor data from vehicles, traffic lights and overhead sign gantries to generate specific recommendations for road users. In less than 100 milliseconds, the AI system creates a digital picture of the situation and derives recommendations from it.

                  10/9/2026
                  Reading time: 3 Min.

                  Climate change is creating new ecosystems worldwide

                  “We need to redefine our conservation goals”

                  Climate change is reshaping which plants and animals live together in ecosystems around the world: some species communities are breaking apart, while new ones are forming. A new study from the Technical University of Munich (TUM) shows that these changes also have important implications for conservation and ecological restoration. The researchers argue that it is time to rethink existing goals and take a more deliberate approach to managing nature in transition.

                  Learn more

                  10/8/2026
                  Reading time: 2 Min.

                  Attacks on cameras and sensors

                  Protecting autonomous vehicles against cyberattacks

                  Driver-assistance systems are now installed in nearly all modern cars. As a result, safety depends on whether computer systems can reliably process sensor data. Researchers at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) are therefore investigating how these systems can be specifically manipulated - with the goal of better protecting vehicles from such attacks in the future. Even a film discreetly placed over the sensors can cause serious accidents.

                  Reading time: 2 Min.

                  Robotics start-up offers a simple solution for industrial manufacturing

                  RobCo is TUM’s 24th unicorn

                  The Munich-based robotics start-up RobCo is the latest unicorn to emerge from the Technical University of Munich (TUM). Founded six years ago by three former robotics students from the TUM Chair of Robotics, Artificial Intelligence and Real-Time Systems, the company focuses on AI-powered automation solutions for industrial manufacturing. It is now the 24th unicorn to emerge from TUM’s entrepreneurship ecosystem.

                  Research for Next-Generation Biomanufacturing

                  TUM and Sartorius establish Cell Culture Biomanufacturing Lab

                  Cell culture technologies are becoming increasingly important for the production of biopharmaceuticals. Against this background, the new TUM Sartorius Cell Culture Biomanufacturing Lab (CCBM) aims to provide state-of-the-art research infrastructure and a shared platform for interdisciplinary research, education, and collaboration with industry.

                  Award for the first detection of cosmic Neutrinos

                  Nobel Prize in physics for TUM Distinguished Affiliated Professor Francis Halzen

                  Belgian-American particle physicist and TUM Distinguished Affiliated Professor Francis Halzen has been awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics. He is being honored for his pioneering work in neutrino astronomy and the first detection of these cosmic particles with the vast IceCube detector at the South Pole, as the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced in Stockholm.

                  10/5/2026

                  Center for Organoid Systems (COS)

                  TUM opens new center for mini-organs

                  A new, innovative biotechnology research center has opened on the Garching campus of the Technical University of Munich (TUM): The Center for Organoid Systems (COS) significantly strengthens the Munich Institute for Biomedical Engineering (MIBE). The organoids studied there reproduce organ functions with a high degree of accuracy. They provide new insights into the human body and lay the groundwork for new therapies for common diseases such as diabetes, cancer, and cardiovascular disease.
