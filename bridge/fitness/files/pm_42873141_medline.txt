TI  - Progressive Overload and Muscle Hypertrophy: Its Relation to Muscle Use in 
      Training Session.
LID - 10.1519/JSC.0000000000005659 [doi]
AB  - Kassiano, W, Manske, I, Miguel, A, Dias, RNdS, Siqueira, JP, Dantas, L, Prado, A, 
      Costa, B, and Cyrino, ES. Progressive overload and muscle hypertrophy: Its 
      relation to muscle use in training session. J Strength Cond Res XX(X): 000-000, 
      2026-To investigate the effects of progressive overload on skeletal muscle 
      hypertrophy and its relationship with muscle use during training. The study 
      comprised 2 components: a training intervention (part 1) and 2 acute resistance 
      exercise bouts (part 2). Forty-one untrained young women had each arm randomized 
      to progressive overload (n = 29), no-progressive overload (N-PO, n = 23), or a 
      time-matched nonexercise control (Ctrl, n = 30). In part 1, subjects performed 
      unilateral elbow flexion (3 sets of 8-12 repetitions, 3 d.wk-1) for 8 weeks. 
      Progressive overload (PO) increased load when the upper repetition limit was 
      reached, whereas N-PO maintained constant load and repetitions. Elbow flexor 
      muscle thickness was measured pre- and post-training at 60 and 70% of arm length 
      and summed ( summation operatorEF). In part 2, subjects performed an acute exercise bout at pre- 
      and post-testing. Muscle thickness was measured before and immediately after 
      exercise as a proxy for muscle use. Both training conditions increased muscle 
      thickness vs. Ctrl, with greater increases in PO than N-PO. Acute thickness 
      increases were similar between conditions at pretesting, but at post-testing were 
      greater in the Ctrl and N-PO than PO, with no differences between Ctrl and N-PO. 
      Progressive overload augments muscle hypertrophy, likely by maintaining higher 
      muscle use during exercise.
